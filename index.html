Research '26

Hybrid Machine Learning-Based SIEM Framework for Real-Time Cyber Threat Detection

Manojkumar Gurram

83.77%F1 under distribution shift
0.69%false-positive rate
3model paradigms fused

A detection framework that combines XGBoost (known attacks), Isolation Forest (statistical anomalies) and a lightweight GraphSAGE (relational anomalies between users, hosts and IPs). Their scores are fused into one interpretable risk score, explained with SHAP, graph masking and an LLM, and indexed into an ELK-based SIEM for analyst triage in Kibana.

Abstract

Supervised intrusion detectors are accurate on attacks they have seen but miss novel ones, while deep-learning alternatives are often too heavy for real-time SOC use. This work asks whether a computationally light, heterogeneous ensemble can stay competitive and remain explainable.

The framework runs three complementary models in parallel and fuses their normalized outputs with a weighted linear combination, chosen over stacking because analysts can see exactly how much each model contributed. Every flagged event carries model-level evidence and a plain-language explanation. Evaluated with held-out attack families to simulate zero-day conditions, the ensemble reaches an F1 of 83.77% at a 0.69% false-positive rate, ahead of XGBoost alone (82.77%, 0.72%), while matching it under stable conditions.

Contributions
  • A three-paradigm ensemble where each model covers another's blind spot: what XGBoost misses, Isolation Forest or GraphSAGE can still flag.
  • An interpretable fusion layer that keeps each model's contribution visible to the analyst.
  • A layered explainability pipeline: SHAP for tabular features, gradient-based masking for graph neighbours, and LLM-written explanations.
  • Unseen-attack evaluation by withholding attack families, plus deployment design for ELK with Kibana triage dashboards.
